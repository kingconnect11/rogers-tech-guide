Harbor Health: your pitch workbook

Prepared by Roger King for Alex Rivera, ahead of demo day on 4 December.

This workbook is built from what you told me about Harbor Health. Do one section a day with a pencil. Bring it to our session, or to the room on demo day.

You told me

Software that fills last-minute cancelled appointments at independent clinics, by texting patients on a waitlist. Seed stage, early revenue. Raising $1.5 million.

Your next pitch

Demo day, 4 December. A five-minute slot, then three minutes of questions, in front of about 60 angels.

Made-up sample for illustration.

Section 1

Your audience

A demo day room is mostly angels, many of them former operators. At seed, in healthcare software, they are listening for four things:

  1. Do clinics actually pay, and how quickly do they decide?
  2. Is this a company, or a feature that a bigger scheduling vendor adds next year?
  3. Why you? What do you know about clinics that most people do not?
  4. What will this round buy, and what does the company look like when it is spent?

Exercise

Write the one sentence you want an angel to repeat at dinner tonight.

Section 2

Your first 30 seconds

You have about 30 seconds before an investor decides whether to lean in. Here is the opening you sent me:

"Harbor Health is an AI-powered patient engagement platform transforming the healthcare scheduling experience."

I hear a sentence like this ten times a night. It names a category, not a company, and it gives me nothing to remember. A strong opening answers four things in plain words: what goes wrong, what you do about it, the proof, and who you are.

Here is one way it could sound, using the numbers you gave me:

"Every day, an independent clinic loses about three appointments to last-minute cancellations. Harbor Health fills them by texting patients on the waitlist. Across our 40 clinics, we fill six in ten of those empty slots. I'm Alex. I ran scheduling for a 12-clinic group for six years."

Now write yours

What goes wrong (one sentence). What you do (one sentence). Your proof (one number). Who you are (one sentence). Read it aloud with a timer. Under 30 seconds?

Section 3

Problem and solution: get to the point

The most common mistake I see: four minutes on the problem and thirty seconds on the solution. In a five-minute slot, the problem gets one minute at most. Then show me what you built and that it works.

Section 4

The three questions you will be asked

These are the questions I would ask Harbor Health. Answer each in two sentences or less. Short answers sound confident; long ones sound rehearsed.

1. "Clinics are slow to buy software. How long does a sale take, and who signs?"

2. "What stops the big scheduling vendors from adding this as a feature?"

3. "Six in ten slots filled: over how many clinics, and how many months?"

If you cannot answer one of these yet, that is the most useful thing this workbook can tell you. Bring it to our session.

Section 5

Your ask

Say the ask in one breath: how much, on what terms, to reach what milestone, by when.

"We are raising $1.5 million on a SAFE to reach [milestone] in [months]."

Not sure about the instrument or the size of the round? That is a numbers question. Michael DeBoard, our fractional CFO, covers it in the Full Program.
