Pitch an Angel

The Full Program

Your pitch, your deck and your numbers, checked by four people before your first investor meeting.

For founders preparing a serious raise who want every part of it ready: how you pitch, what your deck shows, and whether your numbers and the structure of your round hold up to an investor's questions. Six to eight weeks, ten sessions.

Who you work with

Your pitch

Roger King

Founder of Bay Angels in 1997, with hundreds of pitches heard since. Four one-hour sessions on story, delivery, investor questions and a final run-through, with a written report after each.

Your deck

Philip King

A slide-by-slide review of design, energy and phrasing, with a PR view and a read on today's angel market. Then a redesign of up to 15 slides with one revision, a walk-through, and a check-in.

Your numbers

Michael DeBoard

A fractional CFO for early-stage startups. A review of your pricing strategy, a health check of your books with a clean-up plan, and the structure of your raise.

Your rehearsal

The panel

To finish, you pitch to Roger, Philip, Michael and George Parrish the way an angel screening committee would hear you, and receive four scorecards.

Week by week

Week 1

Roger, session 1: you pitch in full and get a first, honest read. Philip, session 1: the slide-by-slide review. Michael, session 1: pricing and the books (send him your latest profit and loss statement beforehand).

Week 2

Roger, session 2: the story and your first 30 seconds. Philip starts the redesign.

Week 3

Michael, session 2: the structure of the raise: how much, on what instrument, what the money buys, and how long it lasts. Second payment due.

Week 4

Philip, session 2: walk through the redesigned deck. You get one round of revisions.

Week 5

Roger, session 3: investor questions, drilled with the new deck.

Week 6

Roger, session 4: the final run-through, timed to your real slot.

Week 7

The mock angel panel, with written scorecards from all four panelists.

Week 8

Philip, session 3: a check-in once your changes are in.

If your raise starts sooner, we compress this into four weeks.

What you will have at the end

  • A pitch you can deliver in five, ten or twenty minutes.
  • A redesigned deck of up to 15 slides.
  • A written report after each of Roger's sessions.
  • A review of your pricing and a plan to clean up your books.
  • A raise you can explain: the amount, the instrument, the use of funds and your runway.
  • Four scorecards from a panel that has heard a great many pitches.
