Pitch to an Angel: the course

Eight lessons of about ten minutes, recorded once and sold many times, each with a page in the founder's personal workbook.

Your time

The eight lessons

Every lesson comes from something you already teach: your five rules, your question set, and your two published articles. Nothing new to invent.

Lesson 1

The goal is a second meeting

Why a pitch exists. Nobody writes a check in the room; the win is a second conversation. What that changes about everything you say.

Workbook: "The one sentence I want them to remember." From: your belief that the purpose of a pitch is to generate interest.

Lesson 2

Know your audience

Angels are not VCs, and a demo day is not a partner meeting. Who is in the room, at your stage, and what each of them listens for.

Workbook: "Who exactly will hear this pitch?" From: rule 1, know the audience.

Lesson 3

The first thirty seconds

Start with the person who has the problem, say what your solution actually does, and explain what the customer uses today. The opening exercise, done live.

Workbook: rewrite your opening. From: your article "Help investors understand your startup in the first thirty seconds".

Lesson 4

Get to the solution

The most common mistake: four minutes on the problem and thirty seconds on the solution. How to balance them, and why your strongest proof goes early.

Workbook: time your problem and your solution. From: rule 3, connect the basics.

Lesson 5

Lead with your strongest proof

Whatever your biggest bragging point is, spend the most time there. Traction, a customer quote, a number, a team that has done it before.

Workbook: "My three strongest proof points, in order." From: rule 2, create interest and earn the follow-up.

Lesson 6

Get the timing right

Three, five, ten and twenty-minute versions. What gets cut first, and how to protect the ending so you never lose the ask to the clock.

Workbook: your timing plan. From: your article "How to pitch your startup to investors: get the timing right".

Lesson 7

Deliver with command

If an investor can read the slide faster than you can say it, they stop listening to you. Eye contact, pace, and composure when someone pushes back mid-sentence.

Workbook: "Pitch it once without looking at the screen." From: rules 4 and 5, deliver with command and use the deck as support.

Lesson 8

The ask, and the hard questions

How to say the ask in one breath. The questions every angel asks: why you and why now, what if an incumbent copies you, what the money buys. Short answers sound confident.

Workbook: your three hardest questions, answered in two sentences each. From: your question set.

A recording morning

9:30

10:00 to 12:30

Four lessons, about ten minutes each, with a break between. Talk as if one founder were across the table. No script. If you stumble, keep going; Philip cuts it.
